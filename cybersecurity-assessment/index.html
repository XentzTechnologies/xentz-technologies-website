Why it matters

Client trust depends on more than tax expertise.

CPA and tax firms work with sensitive tax returns, financial records, identification information and confidential client communications. A small practice may have solid accounting procedures but still rely on informal technology habits that are difficult to document or verify.

Common questions arise when a partner reviews the firm's written security plan, answers an insurance questionnaire, or asks whether a backup could actually restore the files needed during filing season. An independent review helps turn those questions into a concrete list of actions.

Our approach to CPA firm cybersecurity in Dallas is deliberately practical: identify what exists, verify it where possible, and explain the gaps that matter most to your business.

Questions worth being able to answer

  • Is multifactor authentication enforced for every important account?
  • Can former employees still access sensitive information?
  • Are laptops encrypted and critical systems updated?
  • Have backups been restored successfully, not just scheduled?
  • Does our WISP reflect how we actually operate?

Scope of work

What our accounting-firm IT security assessment reviews

Every engagement begins with a documented scope. We review approved configurations and evidence rather than interrupting production systems. The standard review covers six areas important to day-to-day confidentiality and continuity.

01

Identity & Microsoft 365

MFA enforcement, administrator permissions, old accounts, secure sign-in settings, mailbox forwarding and access to shared client files. We look for gaps that could expose email or cloud documents.

02

Computers & devices

Inventory coverage, supported operating systems, security updates, encryption, endpoint protection and unnecessary administrator access. The goal is a clear picture of devices used for sensitive work.

03

Backup & recovery

Critical file coverage, backup separation, monitoring, documented recovery expectations and evidence of restore testing. A successful backup job is not the same thing as a proven recovery process.

04

Network & remote access

Firewall practices, Wi-Fi separation, remote access protections, relevant domain settings and known external exposure. Any active checks are limited to assets and methods specifically authorized in writing.

05

Vulnerability readiness

Patch procedures, aging software, known vulnerabilities and the process used to assign responsibility for fixes. Optional scanning requires explicit scope, authorization and a suitable maintenance window.

06

WISP & governance

Written policies, assigned security responsibilities, staff training, vendor oversight, incident planning and data-handling practices. We show where existing documentation needs attention.

Tax preparer security planning

WISP for tax preparers: a practical readiness check

For covered tax preparation businesses, the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314) requires a written information security program appropriate to the business. The IRS publishes Publication 5708 to help tax professionals organize a Written Information Security Plan (WISP).

The FTC Safeguards Rule for tax preparers addresses safeguards such as access controls, employee training, risk management and oversight of service providers. Specific obligations and exemptions depend on the firm's circumstances, including relevant thresholds under the Rule.

Xentz reviews whether policies and supporting practices are documented and consistent with observed operations. We identify gaps for the firm and its legal or compliance advisers to evaluate. We do not issue a compliance certification or legal interpretation.

A WISP is a working document.

It should tell the firm who is responsible, where sensitive information is handled, which protections are in place and how problems are escalated.

  • Written responsibilities and oversight
  • Risk review and protective controls
  • Data inventory and vendor considerations
  • Security training and incident preparation
  • Review and update practices

A checklist helps guide questions; it cannot establish legal compliance on its own.

Frequently asked questions

What CPA firm owners usually ask

Does a tax preparation firm need a written information security plan?

Tax preparation firms covered by the FTC Safeguards Rule must maintain a written information security program. IRS Publication 5708 offers a WISP planning resource. The particular requirements and exemptions depend on the firm and the information it maintains.

Is this assessment a penetration test?

No. It is a limited review of policies, configurations and evidence. It does not involve exploitation, password attacks, social engineering or penetration testing.

Will you need our passwords?

No. We never request staff passwords. Reviews rely on client-led screen sharing, approved read-only access and evidence supplied by the client.

How long does the assessment take?

We plan for approximately two weeks from kickoff for a standard engagement. The schedule depends on timely access to the agreed evidence and stakeholders.

Do we need to replace our existing IT provider?

No. Your current IT provider can use the recommendations. Implementing fixes is outside the assessment's standard scope.

What does a cybersecurity assessment cost?

We propose a fixed fee after reviewing the firm's size, sites, user count and technical scope. A free 30-minute introductory call helps define what is appropriate.

Will the report make us compliant with the FTC Safeguards Rule?

No. The report identifies readiness gaps and is not a compliance audit, certification, legal opinion or guarantee. Your firm and advisers retain responsibility for compliance decisions.

Will this help lower our cyber insurance costs?

The assessment can identify safeguards that appear in common insurance questionnaires, but no insurer's coverage decision, renewal, eligibility or premium is guaranteed.

What happens to information reviewed during the engagement?

We limit collection to what is necessary, use approved secure methods and handle any evidence under the signed agreement's confidentiality, access, retention and deletion terms.
